# **HOW TO BE SUCCESSFUL**

#### **Session Awareness**

Make sure you’re tracking and trading at the right times. Mark highs and lows of Asia and London to create your trading zones. Wait for pre-market volatility to settle and ignore noise.

- Track Asia (6PM \- 12AM EST)
- Watch London (12AM \- 6AM EST)
- Trade After NY Open (930AM EST)

#### **Entry Confirmation**

Keep it simple \- at Smart Trading Blueprint we are all about simplifying your trading strategy. You are only going to follow 3 entry patterns:

- Inverted Fair Value Gaps
- Change of Delivery
- Market Structure Shifts

#### **Manage Risk Effectively**

Follow these risk management rules religiously:

- 2:1 Reward Minimum
- 1R When Trading Pro Trend, 0.5R When Market is Choppy
- Close Same Day \- NO Overnight Positions

#### **Position Sizing**

Knowing WHAT to trade is one thing, knowing HOW MUCH to trade is another. Make sure you’re following strict position sizing rules to minimize risk and maximize gains.

- NO Overtrading
- Let Winners Run
- Scale Position Size With Your Account

#### **Trading Psychology**

Your mindset as a trader will be more than half the battle \- we promise you that. Practice these trading psychology principles regularly. You must master your mind before you can master the market.

- Master ONE Setup
- Trust Your Levels
- Patience Always Beats FOMO

# **TRADING GLOSSARY**

#### **Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL)**

Liquidity in the form of stop losses on short positions, buy stops, or an area where breakout buyers will purchase. This is where large institutions often sell due to the ability to do so. Buy-Side Liquidity is found above swing highs.

#### **Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)**

Liquidity in the form of stop losses on long positions, sell stops, or an area where breakout sellers will short. This is where large institutions often buy due to the ability to do so. Sell-Side Liquidity is found under swing lows.

#### **Break Even (BE)**

When a trader moves their stop loss order to entry to eliminate the risk of loss.

#### **Displacement**

A clear and sharp move in the market, showing aggressive buying or selling. Creates fair value gaps (FVG) in the process.

#### **Break of Structure (BOS)**

When price breaks a previous high or low in market structure with displacement. Breaks of Structure occur in continuations.

#### **Market Structure Shift (MSS)**

Market structure shifts occur when the market reverses from making lower highs and lower lows, to making a higher high with displacement.

Or vice versa, when the market reverses from making higher highs and higher lows, to making a lower low with displacement.

#### **Equal Highs (EQH)**

Large amounts of buy-side liquidity rest here, due to retail traders looking at this as a sell wall or resistance. Also known as “double tops”.

#### **Equal Lows (EQL)**

Large amounts of sell-side liquidity rest here, due to retail traders looking at this as a buy wall or support. Also known as “double bottoms”.

#### **Fair Value Gap (FVG)**

3-candle formation in which the wicks on the outside candles are spread apart due to an expansive body on the middle candle. We look at these areas as likely magnets to price and possible reversal points due to the fact that there are likely orders remaining in these areas, as it takes aggressive buying or selling to create a FVG.

#### **Higher Low (HL)**

In a bullish trend, we see a series of higher highs and higher lows in price swings.

#### **Higher High (HH)**

In a bullish trend, we see a series of higher highs and higher lows in price swings.

#### **Lower Low (LL)**

In a bearish trend, we see a series of lower highs and lower lows in price swings.

#### **Lower High (LH)**

In a bearish trend, we see a series of lower highs and lower lows in price swings.

#### **High Time Frame (HTF)**

Any time frame higher than the one you’re viewing.

#### **Low Time Frame (LTF)**

Any time frame lower than the one you’re viewing.

#### **London Session**

The London session occurs between Midnight and 6 am ET.

#### **Market Structure**

The series of Highs or Lows that price makes. Can be bearish or bullish.

#### **New York Session**

The New York Session begins at 6am ET, but the Stock market opens at 9: 30 \- this is where the highest quality trades are found for the futures markets.

#### **Order Block (OB)**

An order block is a defined area where buyers or sellers of smart money entered the market & moved price away from its price level to a new area of interest. These levels will be valuable at a later time.

#### **Optimal Trade Entry (OTE)**

The 0.62 \- 0.79 retracement of a leg in price. Fibonacci tools are used to visualize this retracement.

#### **Previous Daily High (PDH)**

The high of the previous trading day. High amounts of buy-side liquidity rest here.

#### **Previous Daily Low (PDL)**

The low of the previous trading day. High amounts of sell-side liquidity rest here.

Time Based Liquidity  
The high or low of previous trading weeks, days, or sessions. These levels are important due to the high amount of liquidity beyond them, and we use them to watch for responses to confirm trades. In these areas the market will give quality setups.

Key Level  
Key levels are found in the form of higher time frame FVGs and time based liquidity.

#### **Risk to Reward (RR)**

The risk/reward ratio marks the prospective reward an investor can earn for every dollar they risk on an investment.

#### **Currency Pair**

It is the quotation of one currency unit against another currency unit. For example, EUR/USD.

#### **Exchange Rate**

It is the rate at which you exchange one currency for another. The exchange rate shows how much of the quote currency you need if you want to buy 1 unit of the base currency.

#### **Quote**

It is a market price that always consists of 2 figures: the bid/selling price, and the ask/buying price.

#### **Account Currency**

It is the currency you choose when you open a trading account.

#### **Pip**

A pip is the smallest price change of a given exchange rate. It’s typically measured by the last decimal point.

#### **Fractional Pip**

An extra decimal place in the exchange rate.

#### **Lot**

Forex is traded in amounts called lots. A standard lot has 100,000 units of the base currency, while a micro lot has 1,000 units.

#### **Pip Value**

The pip value shows how much 1 pip is worth. It changes with market movements.

#### **Leverage**

Leverage is the amount of money a broker lends you to trade larger positions.

#### **Equity**

The total amount of money in your trading account, including your profit and losses.

#### **Position**

It is a trade that you hold open during a certain period of time.

#### **Profit/Loss Calculation**

The calculation of profit or loss when closing a position.

#### **Long Position**

When you enter a long position, you buy a base currency.

#### **Short Position**

When you enter a short position, you sell a base currency.

#### **Close a Position**

When you exit a trade to secure profits or minimize losses.

#### **Swing Trading**

In contrast to day trading, swing trading requires overnight hold times.

#### **Stock Market Hours**

The market is open from 9:30 am to 4 pm EST, Monday to Friday.

#### **Bull or Bullish**

A strong market where stocks are moving up.

#### **Bear or Bearish**

A weak market where stocks are expected to go down.

#### **Initial Public Offering (IPO)**

When a company sells a fixed number of shares to the public to raise money.

#### **Float**

Refers to the number of outstanding shares available to trade.

#### **Stocks Splits**

Stock splits can change the price of a stock and its float.

#### **Scaling in or Scaling out**

Techniques for entering or exiting a position in parts.

#### **Cost Average**

The average price paid for a stock.

#### **Dollar Cost Averaging**

A strategy where you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price fluctuations.

#### **Averaging Down or Averaging Up**

A method of adjusting the cost basis of your position by adding more shares.

#### **Covering**

To close a short position by buying back the shares.

#### **Short Interest**

The number of shares held as a short position.

#### **Short Squeeze**

When a stock price rises and forces traders who are short to cover their positions.

#### **Short Sale Restriction**

Occurs when a stock drops 10% or more in a day.

#### **Cash Account**

A trading account where you can only trade with the funds you deposit.

#### **Margin Accounts**

Accounts where brokers lend you money to trade, using your funds as collateral.

#### **Proprietary Firm Accounts**

Trading firms that allow traders to trade with the firm's capital.

#### **Margin**

The amount of money borrowed from a broker to open a position.

#### **Buying Power**

Your available funds for trading, including margin.

#### **Leverage Rate**

The rate that your cash deposit is multiplied to give you total buying power.

#### **Margin Call**

Occurs when your account equity falls below the required margin level.

#### **Bid Price**

The price traders are willing to buy at.

#### **Ask Price**

The price traders are willing to sell at.

#### **Level 1 Data**

The current bid and ask prices.

#### **Spread**

The difference between the bid and ask prices.

#### **Volume**

The total number of shares traded in a given time period.

#### **Relative Volume**

The volume of a stock compared to its average volume for the same period.

#### **Market Orders**

Orders that are filled at the current market price.

#### **Slippage**

The difference between the expected price of a trade and the price it actually executed at.

#### **Open Order**

An order to buy or sell that remains open until it is filled or canceled.

#### **Limit Order**

An order placed at a specific price, to buy below or sell above the current price.

#### **Stop-Entry Order**

An order to buy or sell when the price reaches a specified level.

#### **Execution**

The process of completing an order.

#### **Take profit Order (TP)**

An order that closes your position when it reaches a set profit level.

#### **Stop-Loss Order (SL)**

An order to close your trade at a set loss level.

#### **Fok Order**

Fill or Kill order, where the entire order must be filled or canceled.

#### **Gtc Order**

Good Till Canceled order, which remains open until canceled.

#### **Fill Price or Getting Filled**

The price at which your order is executed.

#### **Partial Fill**

When only part of an order is filled.

#### **Fundamental Analysis**

Analysis based on economic and financial factors.

#### **Technical Analysis**

Analysis based on price movements and market indicators.

#### **Line Charts**

Simple charts that plot price movement over time.

#### **Bar Charts**

Charts that show the open, high, low, and close prices.

#### **Candlestick Charts**

Charts that display open, high, low, and close prices in a candlestick format.

#### **Chart Time Frames**

The different time intervals used
